It kept rewriting me.

I built MrGrammar because every AI tool I tried kept rewriting me. I would paste a sentence into ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude to fix a typo and get back something polished, formal and not mine.

All I wanted was the typo fixed.

So I made a tool that does only that. It corrects spelling, grammar and punctuation and leaves your words, your tone and your sentence structure exactly as you wrote them. If it sounded like you before, it sounds like you after.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes, while MrGrammar is in early access. There is no plan to upgrade to and no key to bring. The free service allows 10 corrections a minute and 200 a day per person, which is plenty for a day of email and chat. If that ever changes, early users will hear about it first.

What does it change, and what does it leave alone?

It fixes spelling, grammar and punctuation. It does not rewrite your sentences, swap your words or change your tone.

Where does it work?

Gmail, Outlook on the web, Slack, LinkedIn messages and comments, Facebook and Messenger are supported out of the box, plus most websites with ordinary text boxes, comment fields or editors. It runs in Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers.

How is MrGrammar different from other grammar tools?

It only corrects. Most AI writing tools rewrite your sentence, change the tone or suggest a different word. MrGrammar fixes the spelling, grammar and punctuation and leaves everything else as you wrote it. It is also free, needs no account, and puts the fix straight into the text box you are typing in.

Is MrGrammar an AI grammar checker?

Yes. Corrections come from Google Gemini, with Groq as a backup, through a small server the project runs. The extension holds no keys and you do not need your own.

Does it work in Google Docs?

Not yet. Docs draws its text on a canvas rather than as regular page text, so there is nothing for the extension to read. You will see a short message if you try.

What happens to my text?

Nothing leaves your browser until you ask for a correction. At that moment the selected text goes over an encrypted connection to a small server, which asks Google Gemini for the correction, with Groq as a backup. Your text is never stored or logged. The server only keeps a request count per network address for up to 24 hours to enforce the free limit, and that count is never linked to any text. Full privacy policy.

Can I change the keyboard shortcut?

Yes. Open the extension popup and choose Change, or go to chrome://extensions/shortcuts and set any combination you like.

Something did not work. Where do I report it?

Open an issue on GitHub. Bug reports and feature ideas are both welcome.
